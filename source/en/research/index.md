## Current Status

I'm currently in the pre-enrollment preparation phase, **with my specific research direction still being decided through conversations with potential advisors**. Rather than rushing to pin a label on it, I'd rather first understand what a research group works on and needs, then find the best fit given my technical background.

But one thing is certain: **I want my research to have a computational component.**

## What I Bring to a Research Group

Traditional methods of geological engineering, augmented with modern computational tools, yield more than the sum of their parts. Here's what I can get started on immediately:

### Data Processing & Visualization
- Python (NumPy / Pandas / Matplotlib) for experimental data
- Interactive visualizations (D3.js / Three.js / Plotly)
- Database management for structured long-term observation data

### Modeling & Simulation
- Mathematical modeling: abstracting physical problems into algorithms
- Numerical simulation scripts (finite difference, Monte Carlo, etc.)
- Fundamentals of machine learning for classification, regression, pattern recognition

### Engineering Capability
- Building **reproducible, shareable** research tools for the group
- Git-based version control for traceable code and data
- Packaging research outcomes as web visualizations for presentations and outreach

## Directions I Find Interesting

> These are areas I've found compelling after reading the literature — **presented as open-ended self-disclosure, not commitments**. The final direction will follow my advisor's projects.

- **Geohazard prediction & early warning** — improving our ability to anticipate landslides, subsidence, and more
- **Digital & visual geology** — making traditional geological maps and survey data "come alive"
- **Rock mechanics & numerical simulation** — understanding rock behavior under complex conditions via computation
- **Geology × machine learning** — surfacing patterns in geological data that elude human intuition

## Three Goals for Graduate School

1. **Build a solid geological foundation** — being cross-disciplinary is no excuse to slack on the fundamentals; I'll work even harder
2. **Produce at least one meaningful outcome** — a paper, an open-source tool, or a method actually used by my group
3. **Transition from "student" to "researcher"** — able to independently pose questions, design methods, and verify answers
